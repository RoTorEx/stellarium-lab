Jupiter · two main cloud belts

The two broad equatorial belts are a realistic target for a well-focused 102 mm telescope in steady air, including the stock 10 mm. Fine bands, festoons and the Great Red Spot are conditional; rotation also matters.

Saturn · Cassini Division

Look near the two outer ends of the rings, between rings A and B. It can be detected in small apertures with favorable ring opening and steady air. Our November 2026 scene has thin rings, making it harder; a continuous dark gap is not promised.

Why the largest magnification is not necessarily the best

All powers assume the listed nominal focal lengths, including 1300 mm for MAK 102; they are not measurements of a physical setup. Nominal power = telescope focal length / eyepiece focal length. Exit pupil = aperture / power. The brightness column compares squared exit pupils on MAK 102, assuming equal transmission, for extended objects only. The sky background brightens too, so this does not improve object-to-sky contrast. It is not a contrast or visibility forecast. The manufacturer gives an approximate 2× aperture-in-mm ceiling: 204× for 102 mm, not a target to reach every night. 6 mm produces 216.7× and may only make a dimmer, softer image. A 2× Barlow with the stock 10 mm gives 260×, so it is not the first upgrade recommended here.

The inherited stock profile is 25/10 mm; regional bundles can instead contain 20/10 mm. Check your actual kit. The Baader 32 mm uses its published 26 mm field stop, so its nominal field is 1.146° on the MAK 102: only about 18% wider than the inherited 25 mm, and still smaller than the 1.83° Pleiades catalog extent. Published field stops also take precedence over AFOV/power for the Baader 6 mm. Other fields are AFOV/power approximations. No brand sharpness difference is encoded into screenshots.

Conditions, assumptions & how to read this comparison

Representative Warsaw coordinates and 100 m elevation are used for every image. Bortle 4/5 model your stated observing scenarios, not a measurement of central Warsaw. Each object uses one disclosed observing date shared by every instrument. The binocular has fixed 8× optics and is repeated as the same reference image in both rows. No image is enlarged to make the target fill the circle. The four tripod shortlist entries are MAK 102/AZ Pronto, MAK 127/AZ5, Explorer 130PS/AZ5 and Startravel 102/AZ Pronto. Mount labels describe proposed kits; stability and observing posture are not simulated. Explorer and Heritage 130P have the same nominal 130/650 optical specification.

PowerSeeker’s 4 mm eyepiece is 175× with an estimated 30° apparent field. It may exceed useful power under real conditions. Existing field-stop settings for the Sky-Watcher eyepieces are unmeasured. Seeing, optical aberrations, hand shake, eye relief, alignment and dark adaptation are not simulated as an eyepiece-visibility forecast.

The planet’s size and phase, the Moon’s phase and Saturn’s ring tilt are date-dependent. Twilight and moonlight are listed separately from artificial skyglow. Galaxies and nebulae can show photographic textures in Stellarium that greatly exceed what is visible through a small instrument.
