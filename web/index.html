Conditions, assumptions & how to read this comparison

Representative Warsaw coordinates and 100 m elevation are used for every image. Bortle 4/5 model your stated observing scenarios, not a measurement of central Warsaw. Each object uses one disclosed observing date shared by every instrument. The binocular has fixed 8× optics and is repeated as the same reference image in both rows. No image is enlarged to make the target fill the circle. The four tripod shortlist entries are MAK 102/AZ Pronto, MAK 127/AZ5, Explorer 130PS/AZ5 and Startravel 102/AZ Pronto. Mount labels describe proposed kits; stability and observing posture are not simulated. Explorer and Heritage 130P have the same nominal 130/650 optical specification.

PowerSeeker’s 4 mm eyepiece is 175× with an estimated 30° apparent field. It may exceed useful power under real conditions. Existing field-stop settings for the Sky-Watcher eyepieces are unmeasured. Seeing, optical aberrations, hand shake, eye relief, alignment and dark adaptation are not simulated as an eyepiece-visibility forecast.

The planet’s size and phase, the Moon’s phase and Saturn’s ring tilt are date-dependent. Twilight and moonlight are listed separately from artificial skyglow. Galaxies and nebulae can show photographic textures in Stellarium that greatly exceed what is visible through a small instrument.
